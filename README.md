**DriverPass Project Summary**
The DriverPass project involved designing a comprehensive lesson booking and management system for a driving school client. The client required a centralized platform allowing students to seamlessly register and schedule driving lessons, while providing the business with tools to coordinate instructors, vehicles, and calendar availability.

**Project Strengths**
I excelled at visually mapping out the system logic and user flows. Translating raw requirements into detailed UML activity and sequence diagrams ensured that complex decision points—such as handling unavailable lesson time slots—were clearly documented and easy for stakeholders to understand.

**Areas for Revision**
If I could revise one section, I would expand the non-functional requirements in the Business Requirements Document. While the functional features were highly detailed, further quantifying strict metrics around concurrent user scalability, precise load time thresholds, and specific database encryption standards would provide an even stronger architectural blueprint.

**Interpreting User Needs**
I interpreted the user’s needs by breaking down broad stakeholder goals into specific, testable requirements. Prioritizing user needs is critical because developers naturally default to the "happy path" when building. Designing with the actual end-user in mind ensures the final product handles real-world edge cases and prevents frustrating maintenance issues post-launch.

**Future Design Approach**
My approach centers on establishing concrete business requirements before technical design begins. In the future, I will continue to leverage UML modeling as a primary strategy to bridge the gap between business needs and technical execution. Providing solid documentation upfront eliminates guesswork and ensures separate coding and testing teams share a clear, unified vision.
